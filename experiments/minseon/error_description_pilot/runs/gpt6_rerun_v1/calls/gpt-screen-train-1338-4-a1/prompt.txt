QUESTION: My new house has 12 medium ceiling lights but I have seen small and large ceiling lights in other rooms. The small ones require 1 bulb, the medium ones require 2, and the large ones need 3 bulbs. How many bulbs should I buy if my wife says she saw twice as many large ceiling lights as medium ceiling lights and ten more small lights than medium ones?
SOLUTION: Step 1: Define variables for the number of each type of light.
Let M represent the number of medium ceiling lights.
Let L represent the number of large ceiling lights.
Let S represent the number of small ceiling lights.

Step 2: Set up equations based on the information given.
We know:
- M = 12 (since there are 12 medium ceiling lights)
- L = 2M (twice as many large ceiling lights as medium lights)
- S = M + 10 (ten more small lights than medium lights)

Step 3: Substitute the values of M and L into the equation for S.
S = 12 + 10
S = 22

Step 4: Calculate the total number of bulbs needed.
- Small lights: 1 bulb each, so 22 * 1 = 22 bulbs
- Medium lights: 2 bulbs each, so 12 * 2 = 24 bulbs
- Large lights: 3 bulbs each, so 22 * 3 = 66 bulbs

Step 5: Add up all the bulbs.
Total bulbs = 22 + 24 + 66 = 112

#### 112
This is the total number of bulbs you should buy.
