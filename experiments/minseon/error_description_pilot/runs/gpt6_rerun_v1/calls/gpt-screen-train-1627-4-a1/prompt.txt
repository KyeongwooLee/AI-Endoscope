QUESTION: Grace is filling her pool in the backyard with a hose that sprays 50 gallons/hour. She waited for 3 hours but the pool wasn't full, so she decides to add another hose that sprays 70 gallons/hour, and after 2 more hours the pool is full. How much water can Grace’s pool contain?
SOLUTION: Step 1: Determine the amount of water added in the first 3 hours.
- Grace's first hose sprays 50 gallons per hour.
- In 3 hours, the first hose adds \( 50 \text{ gallons/hour} \times 3 \text{ hours} = 150 \text{ gallons} \).

Step 2: Determine the remaining capacity of the pool after the first 3 hours.
- Let \( P \) be the total capacity of the pool.
- After 3 hours, the pool is filled with 150 gallons, so \( P - 150 \text{ gallons} \) is the remaining capacity.

Step 3: Determine the amount of water added in the next 2 hours.
- Grace's second hose sprays 70 gallons per hour.
- In 2 hours, the second hose adds \( 70 \text{ gallons/hour} \times 2 \text{ hours} = 140 \text{ gallons} \).

Step 4: Determine the total capacity of the pool.
- The pool is full after 5 hours (3 hours with the first hose and 2 hours with the second hose).
- Therefore, the total capacity \( P \) is the sum of the remaining capacity after the first 3 hours and the water added in the next 2 hours.
- \( P = 150 \text{ gallons} + 140 \text{ gallons} = 290 \text{ gallons} \).
