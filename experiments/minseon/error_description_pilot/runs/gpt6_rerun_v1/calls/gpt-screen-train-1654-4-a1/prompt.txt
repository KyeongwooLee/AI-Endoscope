QUESTION: Bob is building a garden on his land, and he wants it to be fenced in to keep out varmints. The garden is a rectangular plot 225 feet long by 125 feet wide. He also wants to have a small 3-foot wide gate to walk through and a larger 10-foot wide gate to move his gardening equipment through. How much fencing is Bob going to need to fence in his garden, taking into account the two gates?
SOLUTION: To determine the amount of fencing Bob needs, we first calculate the perimeter of the rectangular plot of land. The formula for the perimeter \( P \) of a rectangle is:

\[
P = 2 \times (length + width)
\]

Substituting the given dimensions:

\[
P = 2 \times (225 + 125) = 2 \times 350 = 700 \text{ feet}
\]

Next, we account for the gates. Each gate is 3 feet wide, so the total width occupied by the gates is:

\[
3 \text{ feet} + 3 \text{ feet} = 6 \text{ feet}
\]

Bob also wants a larger 10-foot wide gate. We add this to the total width occupied by the smaller gate:

\[
6 \text{ feet} + 10 \text{ feet} = 16 \text{ feet}
\]

The total length of the fencing needed is the original perimeter minus the width occupied by the gates:

\[
700 \text{ feet} - 16 \text{ feet} = 684 \text{ feet}
\]

#### 684
Bob is going to need 684 feet of fencing to fence in his garden.
