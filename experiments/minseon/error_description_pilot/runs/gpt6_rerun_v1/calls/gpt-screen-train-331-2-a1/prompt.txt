QUESTION: A roadwork company is paving a newly constructed 16-mile road. They use a mixture of pitch and gravel to make the asphalt to pave the road. Each truckloads of asphalt uses two bags of gravel and five times as many bags of gravel as it does barrels of pitch to make. It takes three truckloads of asphalt to pave each mile of road. The company paved 4 miles of road on one day, then one mile less than double that on the second day. How many barrels of pitch will the company need to finish the remaining road on the third day?
SOLUTION: To solve this problem, we need to break it down into several steps.

1. **Calculate the total length of the road:**
   The road is 16 miles long.

2. **Determine the length of road paved on each day:**
   - On the first day, the company paved 4 miles.
   - On the second day, they paved one mile less than double the first day. One mile less than double the first day is \(2 \times 4 - 1 = 7\) miles.

3. **Calculate the total length of road paved:**
   The total length of road paved is the sum of the lengths paved on each day: \(4 + 7 = 11\) miles.

4. **Determine the remaining length of road to be paved:**
   The remaining length of road to be paved is \(16 - 11 = 5\) miles.

5. **Calculate the number of truckloads needed for the remaining road:**
   It takes three truckloads of asphalt to pave each mile of road. Therefore, for 5 miles of road, the company will need \(3 \times 5 = 15\) truckloads of asphalt.

6. **Determine the number of bags of gravel needed for the remaining road:**
   Each truckload of asphalt uses two bags of gravel. Therefore, for 15 truckloads, the company will need \(15 \times 2 = 30\) bags of gravel.

7. **Determine the number of barrels of pitch needed for the remaining road:**
   The number of barrels of pitch needed is five times the number of bags of gravel used, so \(30 \times 5 = 150\) barrels of pitch.

#### 150
This is the number of barrels of pitch the company will need to finish the remaining road on the third day.
