QUESTION: A store is having an anniversary sale. A tub of ice cream costs $2 less than its original price of $12 and the juice is sold at $2 for 5 cans. How much will you pay if you buy two tubs of ice cream and 10 cans of juice?
SOLUTION: Step 1: Determine the sale price of a tub of ice cream.
- Original price of a tub of ice cream: $12
- Discount: $2

Sale price of a tub of ice cream = Original price - Discount
= $12 - $2
= $10

Step 2: Calculate the total cost of two tubs of ice cream.
- Cost of one tub of ice cream: $10
- Number of tubs: 2

Total cost of two tubs of ice cream = Cost per tub × Number of tubs
= $10 × 2
= $20

Step 3: Calculate the cost of 10 cans of juice.
- Cost per can of juice: $2
- Number of cans: 10

Total cost of 10 cans of juice = Cost per can × Number of cans
= $2 × 10
= $20

Step 4: Calculate the total cost for two tubs of ice cream and 10 cans of juice.
- Total cost of two tubs of ice cream: $20
- Total cost of 10 cans of juice: $20

Total cost = Cost of two tubs + Cost of 10 cans
= $20 + $20
= $40
