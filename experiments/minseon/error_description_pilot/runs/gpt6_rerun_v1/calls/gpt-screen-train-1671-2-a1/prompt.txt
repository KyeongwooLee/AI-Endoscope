QUESTION: Rodney and Todd are rowing down a river that is 50 yards wide at one end. If the river's width increases from this end uniformly by 2 yards every 10 meters along, and they row along the river at a rate of 5 m/s, how long (in seconds) will it take them to get to the point where the river is 80 yards wide?
SOLUTION: To solve this problem, we need to determine the distance Rodney and Todd need to row to reach the point where the river is 80 yards wide.

1. **Determine the distance along the river where the river is 80 yards wide:**
   - The river's width increases uniformly by 2 yards every 10 meters.
   - We need to find the distance where the width reaches 80 yards.
   - Let \( x \) be the distance in meters where the river is 80 yards wide.
   - The width of the river can be expressed as:
     \[
     \text{Width} = 2 \cdot \left(\frac{x}{10}\right)
     \]
   - Set the width equal to 80 yards:
     \[
     80 = 2 \cdot \left(\frac{x}{10}\right)
     \]
   - Solve for \( x \):
     \[
     80 = \frac{2x}{10}
     \]
     \[
     80 \cdot 10 = 2x
     \]
     \[
     800 = 2x
     \]
     \[
     x = \frac{800}{2}
     \]
     \[
     x = 400 \text{ meters}
     \]

2. **Calculate the time to row 400 meters:**
   - Rodney and Todd row at a rate of 5 m/s.
   - Time \( t \) can be calculated using the formula:
     \[
     t = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Speed}}
     \]
   - Substitute the values:
     \[
     t = \frac{400 \text{ meters}}{5 \text{ m/s}}
     \]
     \[
     t = 80 \text{ seconds}
     \]

Rodney and Todd will take 80 seconds to get to the point where the river is 80 yards wide.
