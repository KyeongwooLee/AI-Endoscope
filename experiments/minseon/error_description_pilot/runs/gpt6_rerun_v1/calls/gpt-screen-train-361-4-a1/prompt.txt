QUESTION: On the first day of the journey, the Skipper sailed his ship halfway to the destination by traveling due east for 20 hours at a speed of 30 kilometers per hour, and then turned the ship's engines off to let them cool down. But while the engines were off, a wind storm blew his vessel backward in a westward direction.  After the storm, the Skipper used his GPS device to determine the new location of the ship, and he discovered that he was only one-third of the way to his destination.  How many kilometers had the ship been blown in a westward direction by the storm?
SOLUTION: On the first day, the ship traveled 20 hours at a speed of 30 kilometers per hour. So, the distance covered by the ship is:
\[ \text{Distance}_1 = 20 \times 30 = 600 \text{ kilometers} \]

After the engines were turned off, the ship was blown backward by a wind storm. After the storm, the Skipper discovered that the ship was only one-third of the way to the destination. Let's denote the total distance to the destination as \( D \). Then, the distance covered by the ship after the storm is \( \frac{D}{3} \).

Since the ship had already covered 600 kilometers and then was blown backward by \( D - 600 \) kilometers, the remaining distance is one-third of the total distance \( D \):
\[ D - 600 = \frac{D}{3} \]

To solve for \( D \), we multiply both sides by 3:
\[ 3(D - 600) = D \]
\[ 3D - 1800 = D \]
\[ 2D = 1800 \]
\[ D = 900 \text{ kilometers} \]

So, the total distance to the destination is 900 kilometers. The distance covered by the ship before the storm was 600 kilometers, and it was blown backward by one-third of the total distance, which is:
\[ \frac{900}{3} = 300 \text{ kilometers} \]

Therefore, the ship was blown in a westward direction by the storm for:
#### 300
