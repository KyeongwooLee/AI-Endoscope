QUESTION: Mr. Johnson has a prescription with enough pills for 30 days. After four-fifths of the days, he has 12 pills left. How many pills is Mr. Johnson supposed to take a day if he takes the same dose daily?
SOLUTION: To solve this problem, let's break it down step by step:

1. **Determine the total number of pills Mr. Johnson has:**
   - Mr. Johnson's prescription lasts for 30 days.
   - After 4/5 of the days, he has 12 pills left.
   - This means he has taken pills for 1/5 of the days.
   - Let \( x \) be the number of pills Mr. Johnson takes per day.
   - In 1/5 of the 30 days, he takes \( \frac{1}{5} \times 30 = 6 \) days.
   - Therefore, the total number of pills he has is \( 6x \).

2. **Set up an equation to find \( x \):**
   - The total number of pills Mr. Johnson has is equal to the number of pills he has left plus the number of pills he has taken.
   - So, \( 6x = 12 + x \).

3. **Solve the equation for \( x \):**
   - Subtract \( x \) from both sides: \( 6x - x = 12 \).
   - Simplify: \( 5x = 12 \).
   - Divide by 5: \( x = \frac{12}{5} \).

4. **Calculate the value of \( x \):**
   - \( x = \frac{12}{5} = 2.4 \).

This is the number of pills Mr. Johnson is supposed to take daily.
