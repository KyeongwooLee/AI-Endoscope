QUESTION: Johnny is buying planks of wood to build some tables. Each table needs a plank of wood for each of the legs and 5 planks of wood for the surface. If Johnny wants to build 5 tables, how many planks of wood does he need?
SOLUTION: To determine the total number of planks of wood Johnny needs, let's break down the requirements for one table and then multiply by the number of tables he wants to build.

1. Each table needs 1 plank of wood per leg. Assuming Johnny needs 3 planks for each leg (a typical number for a table), the number of planks needed for the legs of one table is:
   \[
   3 \text{ planks/leg} \times 4 \text{ legs} = 12 \text{ planks}
   \]

2. Each table also needs 5 planks of wood for the surface.

3. Adding the planks needed for the legs and the surface, the total number of planks needed for one table is:
   \[
   12 \text{ planks} + 5 \text{ planks} = 17 \text{ planks}
   \]

4. Johnny wants to build 5 tables, so the total number of planks needed for all 5 tables is:
   \[
   17 \text{ planks/table} \times 5 \text{ tables} = 85 \text{ planks}
   \]

#### 85
Johnny needs 85 planks of wood to build 5 tables.
