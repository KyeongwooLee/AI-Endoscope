QUESTION: A factory decides to stop making cars and start making motorcycles instead. When it made cars, per month, it cost $100 for materials, they could make 4 cars, and they sold each car for $50. Now that they make motorcycles it costs $250 for materials, but they sell 8 of them for $50 each. How much more profit do they make per month selling motorcycles instead of cars?
SOLUTION: Step 1: Calculate the profit from making cars per month.
- Cost per car: $100
- Number of cars made per month: 4
- Selling price per car: $50

Profit per car = Selling price - Cost = $50 - $100 = -$50

Step 2: Calculate the total profit from making cars per month.
- Profit per car: -$50
- Number of cars made per month: 4

Total profit from cars per month = Profit per car * Number of cars = -$50 * 4 = -$200

Step 3: Calculate the profit from making motorcycles per month.
- Cost per motorcycle: $250
- Number of motorcycles sold per month: 8
- Selling price per motorcycle: $50

Profit per motorcycle = Selling price - Cost = $50 - $250 = -$200

Step 4: Calculate the total profit from making motorcycles per month.
- Profit per motorcycle: -$200
- Number of motorcycles sold per month: 8

Total profit from motorcycles per month = Profit per motorcycle * Number of motorcycles = -$200 * 8 = -$1600

Step 5: Calculate the difference in profit between making cars and motorcycles.
- Profit from cars: -$200
- Profit from motorcycles: -$1600

Difference in profit = Profit from motorcycles - Profit from cars = -$1600 - (-$200) = -$1400

#### -1400
The factory makes $1400 less profit per month selling motorcycles instead of cars.
