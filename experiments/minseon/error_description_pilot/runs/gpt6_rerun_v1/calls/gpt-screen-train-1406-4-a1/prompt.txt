QUESTION: Bill is trying to count the toddlers at his daycare, but they keep running around. He double-counts 8 toddlers and misses 3 who are hiding. If Bill thinks he counted 26 toddlers, how many are there really?
SOLUTION: Step 1: Identify the problem
Bill double-counts 8 toddlers and misses 3, and he thinks he counted 26.

Step 2: Correct for double-counting and missing toddlers
Let \( x \) be the number of real toddlers. According to the problem:
- Bill double-counts 8 toddlers, so he counts them 2 times.
- He misses 3 toddlers, so he should have counted them 1 time but didn't.

Step 3: Set up the equation
Bill thinks he counted 26, so:
\[ 2x + 1 \cdot (26 - 8 - 3) = 26 \]

Step 4: Solve for \( x \)
\[ 2x + 1 \cdot 15 = 26 \]
\[ 2x + 15 = 26 \]
