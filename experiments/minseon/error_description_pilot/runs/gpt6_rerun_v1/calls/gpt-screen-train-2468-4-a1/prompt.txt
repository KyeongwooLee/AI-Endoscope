QUESTION: A psychiatrist has 4 patients that need 25 sessions in total. One of the patients needs 6 sessions. Another patient needs 5 more than that. How many sessions would the remaining patients need?
SOLUTION: Let's start by defining the variables:
- Let \( x \) be the number of sessions needed by the patient who needs 6 sessions.
- The patient who needs 5 more sessions than \( x \) is \( x + 5 \).
- The remaining two patients need a total of \( 25 - (x + (x + 5)) = 25 - 2x - 5 = 20 - 2x \) sessions.

Since the total number of sessions needed is 25 and one patient needs 6 sessions, the remaining two patients need \( 25 - 6 = 19 \) sessions. Therefore, we have:
\[ 20 - 2x = 19 \]

Now, let's solve for \( x \):
\[ 20 - 2x = 19 \]
\[ -2x = 19 - 20 \]
\[ -2x = -1 \]
\[ x = \frac{-1}{-2} \]
\[ x = \frac{1}{2} \]

So, the patient who needs 6 sessions is \( x = \frac{1}{2} \).

Now we calculate the sessions needed by the remaining two patients:
\[ 20 - 2x = 20 - 2 \left( \frac{1}{2} \right) = 20 - 1 = 19 \]

Finally, we add the sessions needed by the remaining two patients:
\[ 19 + 19 = 38 \]

#### 38
The answer is 38.
