QUESTION: John has 2 hives of bees.  One of the hives has 1000 bees and produces 500 liters of honey.  The second has 20% fewer bees but each bee produces 40% more honey.  How much honey does he produce?
SOLUTION: Step 1: Determine the number of bees in the second hive.
20% fewer bees means:
\(1000 \times 0.20 = 200\) bees fewer
\(1000 - 200 = 800\) bees in the second hive

Step 2: Calculate the honey production per bee in the second hive.
\(500 \text{ liters} \div 1000 \text{ bees} = 0.5 \text{ liters per bee}\)

Step 3: Calculate the increased honey production per bee in the second hive.
\(0.5 \text{ liters per bee} \times 1.40 = 0.70 \text{ liters per bee}\)

Step 4: Determine the total honey production from the second hive.
\(800 \text{ bees} \times 0.70 \text{ liters per bee} = 560 \text{ liters}\)

Step 5: Calculate the total honey production from both hives.
\(500 \text{ liters} + 560 \text{ liters} = 1060 \text{ liters}\)

#### 1060
The total honey production from both hives is 1060 liters.
